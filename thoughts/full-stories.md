## Sift

Concept projects invite cherry-picking, so I graded every source before it could influence a decision. Verified meant peer-reviewed or replicated. Directional meant a single credible study. Vendor claim meant marketing numbers, treated only as a signal of what companies believe. The grading changed the design: frequency framing rests on Verified research and shipped as a core pattern, while the feedback-framing decision rested on a Directional finding and shipped as a hypothesis Round 1 was built to check.

Both rounds ran unmoderated in Maze on the wired prototype, blocks 1 to 10 identical word for word. Five CX participants per round is enough to expose a broken flow and not enough to estimate an effect size, so 83.5% to 0% is a before-and-after of one redesign, not a controlled experiment. With no production data, the model’s calibration outside my held-out set is untested. Round 3 has a scope: badge affordance on the chips, a copy test on Adjust automation, and one open response suggesting the human-in-the-loop philosophy could be more legible on the surface.

## FiPet

I ran 10 interviews in my first week, intercepting parents and kids in coffee shops and parks so I could watch kids approach the app while parents read what they were seeing. A formal focus group was not realistic in the time available. The pattern was not about the financial content: ten of ten called the app visually overwhelming, and one parent said it felt like homework.

Round 2 launched unmoderated on the coded build with 7 participants. Every one dropped off before finishing: zero reportable data, and one expensive lesson. Unmoderated tests on external prototypes bleed participants at every context switch, and a better build does not fix a recruitment problem. I would rather report that than drop the round.

## Lyft

The intuitive answer is to charge more for docking at understocked stations. I rejected it. Destination-side pricing puts the financial decision at the wrong moment, when the rider has committed and the bike is moving, and rerouting mid-ride to save thirty cents is the phone use the safety constraint rules out. Departure-side pricing clears overstocked stations and frees full docks at once.

Maze counts sessions, not unique people, so counts vary by block: 10 on dock planning, 8 on station comparison, 5 to 10 on the opinion scales. Enough to expose a broken affordance, not enough to claim an effect. Round 2 is scoped to concrete targets: misclick under 25%, comprehension above 80%, confidence held at 4.4.

## Biasly

Moving an indicator from the bottom of a card to the top sounds small. It meant renegotiating the whole hierarchy: image treatment, source placement, card balance. Every stakeholder reacted to the smaller image before they noticed the bias placement. Most of the argument was about consequences, not the change itself.

The sessions measured recognition in a controlled task. They cannot tell me whether reading behaviour changed over weeks, or whether the indicator works outside a moderated room. The spectrum also assumes blue reads as left and red as right, an American convention and a recent one. One annotated card on first use would calibrate that.
